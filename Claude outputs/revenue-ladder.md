# The ladder off the ambulance

## The numbers as given

| | |
| --- | --- |
| 24 hours/week of paramedic work | **$3,000/month net** — about $29/hour |
| current load | 120 hours/week ≈ **$15,000/month** |
| monthly requirement | **$16,000–18,000** |

Those are internally consistent. You are not working 120 hours out of habit;
you are working 120 hours because that is what the bills cost. Any plan that
ignores that is a fantasy, and any plan that demands you replace $18,000 before
you cut a single shift is also a fantasy.

---

## Do not aim at $18,000

Aim at **$3,000**, five times.

Each rung buys back 24 hours a week. Those 24 hours are not rest — they are the
input to the next rung. That is the entire mechanism, and it is why the first
rung is worth more than it looks.

| rung | monthly freelance | ambulance hours | what it buys |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **$3,000** | 120 → 96 | ~100 hours/month to build with |
| 2 | $6,000 | 96 → 72 | another ~100 |
| 3 | $9,000 | 72 → 48 | you are now part-time |
| 4 | $12,000 | 48 → 24 | one shift a week |
| 5 | $16–18,000 | 0 | out |

**Rung 1 is two retainer clients.** That is the whole first target. Not a
business, not a brand — two clients paying $1,500 a month.

---

## Price for five clients, not fifteen

At $1,500/month, $18,000 means **twelve retainer clients**. Solo, that is not a
business you can run — it is twelve relationships, twelve invoices, twelve
people with your number.

At $3,000/month it is **six**. Six is a business one person can run well.

So price high and serve fewer, and use the vertical that supports it. A dental
or medical practice can justify $2,500–3,000/month on arithmetic you can show
them:

> A recovered no-show is worth $150–300. Recover two a week and the system pays
> for itself before you count anything else.

A youth club cannot make that argument. It has no per-appointment revenue and no
budget line. **This is the second reason medical wins, and it is a bigger one
than the demand data.**

| | retainer | clients for $18k | workable solo? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| clubs | $300–800 | 25–60 | no |
| small practices | $1,500–3,000 | 6–12 | yes |

---

## Two revenue shapes, and you need both

**Project** — $2,500–6,000 once. Booking page, site rebuild, intake flow. Pays
the bill this month. Does not repeat.

**Retainer** — $1,500–3,000/month. Maintenance, changes, monitoring, reporting.
Boring, unglamorous, and **it is the only kind of money that lets you drop a
shift**, because you can only quit against income you can predict.

Sell the project first because it is easier to say yes to. Convert to retainer
at the end of it — *"it needs someone watching it; here is what that costs."*
Never deliver a project and walk away.

## Honest timeline

You asked for a plan, so here is the part a plan usually leaves out.

| rung | realistic |
| --- | --- |
| 1 — $3,000 | **2–4 months** |
| 2 — $6,000 | +3–4 months |
| 3 — $9,000 | +4–6 months |
| 5 — out | **18–30 months** total |

That is not pessimism, it is the normal shape of building a service business
from zero clients. The first two clients are the slowest; referrals compound
after that; and every rung hands you back time that makes the next one faster.

If someone tells you a shorter number, they are selling you something.

**The one thing that could genuinely compress it:** stop selling custom work and
sell the same thing repeatedly. You are already building that — the pipeline
from the course is one system pointed at different documents. The tenth booking
system takes a fraction of the first. Productisation is what turns a 30-month
ladder into an 18-month one, and it is the real reason the course work matters.

## What rung 1 actually requires

Two clients at $1,500/month. To get two, assume:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| audits performed | 12–15 |
| audits → discovery calls | maybe half |
| calls → paying clients | maybe 1 in 3 |
| **→** | **~2 clients** |

So: **12–15 patient-access audits.** At 20 minutes each that is five hours of
phone calls, spread over six weeks. Not five hours of "building a business" —
five hours of dialling.

That is the plan. Everything else is support for it.

### Sized to 30-minute blocks

| block | what |
| --- | --- |
| Mon | 2 audit calls |
| Tue | write up both one-pagers, send them |
| Wed | course — the cold audit, then Week 6 |
| Thu | 2 consultant contacts, log in `referral_tracker.py` |
| Fri | follow up anyone at `next_touch` |
| Sat/Sun | 1 build block, 1 audit if the week was thin |

Six blocks. Three hours. That is the whole week, and it fits inside 120 hours of
shifts because it has to.

## Two things to be honest with yourself about

**A slow month is not failure.** With a plan this long, the failure mode is
quitting during a quiet stretch, not a bad month. That is what the conversation
counter is for — it moves when you do the work, whether or not anyone has paid
yet.

**120 hours a week has a ceiling that is not financial.** It is not sustainable
for 18 months, and the plan depends on you still being able to think at month
twelve. Rung 1 matters more for that than for the $3,000.
