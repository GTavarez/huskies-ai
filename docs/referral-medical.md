# Referral outreach — medical practices

The clubs pitch does not transfer. A youth club's problem is *parents asking
questions a document should answer*. A medical practice's problem is *patients
not getting booked*. Same technology underneath, completely different pain, and
a practice manager will switch off the moment you say "document assistant".

The tier split still holds — SCORE and NJSBDC get no commission, private
consultants and CPAs do. Only the offer changes.

## The market, with numbers you can say out loud

From MGMA's December 2025 poll of practice leaders (236 responses), on their
top priority for 2026:

| priority | share of practice leaders |
| --- | --- |
| **no-shows** | **27%** |
| **online scheduling** | **24%** |
| **phone access** | **22%** |
| wait times | 21% |

And the two figures that make the case:

- **No-shows cost roughly $150,000 per physician per year**, about 14% of a
  medical group's revenue on a given day.
- **71% of medical groups have fewer than one in four patients using digital
  tools to schedule.**

Read those together. The top three priorities for 2026 are all appointment
access, and three quarters of practices have almost no digital scheduling in
place. That is a market that has already told a survey it wants this and has
not bought it yet.

This is a much better position than the clubs vertical, where you have to
convince someone a problem exists.

## The free diagnostic — a patient access audit

The clubs version is a document check. The practices version is **a mystery
shopper call**, and it is better, because the finding is undeniable.

Twenty minutes per practice:

1. **Call as a new patient during business hours.** Time it. How long to reach a
   human? Could you book? What is the next available appointment?
2. **Try to book online.** Is there a link? Does it work on a phone? How many
   clicks? Does it actually confirm, or just send a request into a void?
3. **Call after hours.** Voicemail? A message that says call back tomorrow?
   Nothing at all?
4. **Check what happens next.** Confirmation email or text? A reminder before
   the appointment?

Then a one-page snapshot: four numbers and what each one costs them.

Why this beats any pitch: you are not telling them their practice has a
problem, you are telling them **what happened when you called at 2:40 on a
Tuesday**. That is not an opinion and they cannot argue with it.

Do the audit on one practice *before* your first consultant call, so you can
describe a real one instead of describing the idea of one.

## Before you take a single medical client — the HIPAA gate

A consultant will ask. A practice manager will definitely ask. "I hadn't thought
about it" ends the conversation and you do not get a second one.

**A patient's name attached to an appointment is protected health information.**
Scheduling, reminders and intake all touch it. That means:

- You would be a **business associate** and need a signed **BAA** with the
  practice.
- Every vendor in the chain that touches PHI needs a BAA with *you* — the
  scheduling tool, the SMS provider, the hosting, the AI model API. Several
  popular ones will not sign one on a low tier.
- A general-purpose model API with no BAA must never see patient data.

**The clean way in, and it is a real offer, not a dodge:** start with work that
touches no PHI at all.

- the public-facing booking page and the flow into their existing system
- the website and the "request an appointment" path
- reminder *templates* and the schedule for them, with the practice's own
  compliant tool doing the sending
- the access audit itself — you are a member of the public calling a phone
  number

That is genuinely valuable, it is most of what those MGMA numbers are about,
and it lets you start earning before you have the compliance stack. Say so
plainly rather than implying coverage you do not have.

I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice — before you sign anything that
touches patient data, have someone who does this for a living review the BAA and
your vendor chain.

## What you are actually selling here

Not "AI". **Filled appointment slots.**

The clubs pitch sells a measurement. This one sells revenue that is already
leaking — $150,000 per physician per year, in a practice that has told a survey
it wants to fix exactly this and has not.

Lead every conversation with the audit, never with the technology. The
technology is how you deliver; it is not why anyone buys.

---

## Your paramedic licence is not a side note

You are a licensed paramedic pitching medical practices. Most people selling
scheduling software to a practice manager have never been inside a clinical
environment. You have.

Put it in the first line. It is the single fastest credibility signal you own in
this vertical, and you have been leaving it out.
